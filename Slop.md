# My Thoughts on AI Slop Mods on the Workshop

## Why we get slop mods

The side effects of AI tools:

* It's faster to create things with lower effort.
* It lowers the barrier of entry so people can create things beyond their level of skill or comprehension.
* Because of the nature of RimWorld modding, it's easier to write code than it is to write tests.

Slop happens when a modder says "good enough" and ship something that shouldn't have seen the light of day.

Video game mods need a lot of in-game testing. People can use AI to create the mod but most of the testing has to be done manually.

Vibe coding without tests is a bad time on any platform/language because reading/debugging code is harder than writing code.

## What matters is quality

If you are choosing to boycott AI then the existence of AI is enough for you to pass on things and that is fine. I think that's possible right now because we're in the early adoption phase of AI, but I have no illusions that you'll be able to make that distinction in the future (e.g. the author didn't use AI but they used tools that used AI).

If you have any kind of retirement strategy with index funds or mutual funds you are more exposed to AI than if you used some video game mod.

As someone who falls more in the "I need to understand AI to keep my job and feed my family" camp, I think the two big problems with AI are:

1. AI exponentially increases the signal-to-noise problem in all content on all platforms.
2. AI increases the rate-of-change of updates, which reduces stability, which causes no end of problems to end users.

When you look at a mod you want to see that the author is fixing issues vs it being abandoned, but you also want to see some stability and that things are changing at content milestones.

Letting your users find your bugs is a problem that has existed long before AI was introduced to modding.

## How I have a good time with mods in 2026

### Solution 1: Let someone else do the work

There are people doing an insane amount of effort creating and testing mod collections. The Progression discord shows an almost "full time job" level of dedication to creating and maintaining a free mod collection.

* [Ferny](https://steamcommunity.com/id/misterferny/myworkshopfiles/?section=collections&appid=294100) - professionally maintained collection
* [Samuel Streamer](https://steamcommunity.com/id/MrSamuelStreamer/myworkshopfiles/?section=collections&appid=294100) - collections created for their youtube series
* [veequeue](https://steamcommunity.com/id/veequeue1/myworkshopfiles/?section=collections&appid=294100) - collections created for their youtube series
* [MysteriousFawx](https://steamcommunity.com/id/mysteriousfawx/myworkshopfiles/?section=collections&appid=294100) - mod reviewer
* [Regression](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3734979613) - tweaked version of `progression`
  
### Solution 2: Follow Mod Authors

If you like the mods someone makes then FOLLOW THEM ON STEAM. You get a special little section called "mods from authors you follow".

I also strongly recommend using the [Rimworld Workshop](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/workshop/) via web browser rather than inside of Steam. It makes opening tabs and right-click to translate so much easier.

### Solution 3: Aging and Quality Indicators

When you find cool stuff on "latest updates", stick them in a [workshop collection of bookmarked mods to try out](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3569495245).

* Don't count how many mods you've bookmarked. **Never count**
* When you do look at them, check the comments for bugs, was the mod maintained, how many subscriptions it has, how many collections it is in. Quality indicators aren't there on when the mod is first uploaded.

Does it have a github and an open source license? That is a potential sign of quality. If it has source code and a permissible license that means it can be maintained when the original modder switches games.

You can also look at the github history and get some indicator of how long the mod was under development before it was released, as well as seeing how active the author is in general.

## Recommendations for not bricking your save

### Recommendation: RimSort

Use RimSort for organizing your mods. Not RimPy and not in-game mod sorters.

### Recommendation: RimSort mod lists

Use RimSort's save/load mod list features when you want to try out something.

Pro-tip: stick the lists inside of a version control tool if you're a real geek.

### Recommendation: auto-saves

 Keep a lot of autosaves so you can go back farther in time to before a mod issue happened. I use [RimSaves](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1713367505).
